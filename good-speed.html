For Gaming: Ping Matters More Than Speed

Many gamers think they need 500 Mbps to play online games smoothly. In reality, most games only use 3-6 Mbps of bandwidth. What actually causes lag is high ping (latency) and jitter.

For competitive shooters like Valorant, CS2, or Fortnite, aim for a ping below 30ms. If your ping is above 100ms, you'll notice rubber-banding, delayed hits, and disconnections — even on a 1 Gbps connection.

Tip: Use an Ethernet cable instead of Wi-Fi to reduce ping by 10-30ms instantly.

For Streaming: Upload Is King

If you're streaming on Twitch or YouTube Live, your upload speed is what matters. A 1080p stream at 60fps requires at least 6-8 Mbps upload. For 4K streaming, you'll need 20+ Mbps upload.

Most residential internet plans are asymmetric — meaning your upload is much slower than your download. If you stream regularly, look for fiber plans with symmetric speeds (e.g., 300 Mbps down / 300 Mbps up).

For Video Calls: Stability Wins

Zoom, Google Meet, and Teams don't need much bandwidth — 5-10 Mbps is plenty. But they're extremely sensitive to jitter (inconsistent latency). High jitter causes choppy audio, frozen video, and "you're breaking up" moments.

If your video calls keep dropping, run our speed test multiple times. If your ping and jitter vary wildly between tests, your connection is unstable — likely due to Wi-Fi interference or ISP congestion.

How Many Devices Can Your Speed Handle?

Divide your total speed by the number of active devices. A 100 Mbps plan with 5 people streaming simultaneously gives each person ~20 Mbps. That's fine for HD, but 4K will buffer during peak hours.

  • 1-2 devices: 50 Mbps is plenty
  • 3-5 devices: Aim for 100-200 Mbps
  • 6+ devices: Consider 300+ Mbps or fiber
